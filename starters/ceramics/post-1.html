People ask what the number on our labels means. Every Hollowell piece says "cone 8" on the base, and it is the most important thing about how it will behave in your kitchen.

A cone is a thermometer made of glaze

A pyrometric cone is a small pyramid of ceramic material designed to bend at a known amount of heat-work. Not a temperature exactly, but temperature over time, which is what actually matters to clay. We stand three cones in the kiln where we can see them through the spy hole. When cone 7 slumps, we slow down. When cone 8 bends to touch the shelf, we hold. If cone 9 starts to lean, we have gone too far.

Cone 8 works out at roughly 1260°C at our rate of climb. The number itself is less important than the fact that it is the same every time.

Why not lower, and why not higher

Lower is cheaper and softer

A lot of pottery is fired around cone 04, which is closer to 1060°C. It uses less gas and the colours stay bright. It also stays slightly porous, so a mug fired that way will eventually take on the smell of the dishwasher and, if you leave it on a windowsill, it will craze and weep. Fine for a vase. Not fine for the mug you use every morning.

Higher is stronger but meaner

Push a stoneware body to cone 10 and it gets glassier and stronger again, but our glazes start to run and the kiln shelves wear out twice as fast. Cone 8 is where our four glazes sit still, our clay vitrifies fully, and the kiln lives a long life.

What it means for you

  • Everything we sell is fully vitrified. It will not absorb water, tea or coffee.
  • It is dishwasher safe, and has been through ours about a thousand times.
  • Microwaves are fine. The clay does not heat up on its own.
  • A chip, if you get one, is a chip. It will not spread.

The firing takes about fourteen hours from cold, and the kiln then cools for two days before we can open it. That is why a firing comes out on a Thursday and Kiln notes go out on a Thursday night.
