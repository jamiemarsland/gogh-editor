The studio

Two potters, one kiln, and an arch that shakes every eight minutes.

Hollowell started in 2019 when two potters pooled their savings on a second-hand kiln and a lease on Arch 14, Cheese Lane. The first firing was mostly test tiles. The second sold out at a Saturday market.

Today the studio makes about four thousand pieces a year for tables, cafes and a few shops we like. The room is bigger. The kiln is the same.

How a pot gets made

From a bag of clay to your table

Every piece goes through the same four hands and the same four stages. Nothing is cast, nothing is bought in.

1

Throw

Wedged stoneware, centred and pulled up on the wheel. A mug takes about four minutes; a bottle, nearer ten.

2

Trim and handle

A day later, leather-hard, each piece is trimmed on the wheel and handles are pulled and attached by hand.

3

Bisque and glaze

A first firing to 1000°C makes the clay hard enough to dip. Then the glaze, one of four we mix ourselves.

4

Fire

Cone 8 in reduction, about 1260°C over fourteen hours. The kiln cools for two days before we open it.

Every piece is thrown, trimmed, glazed and fired here.

How we work

Small batches, honest seconds, no plastic.

Small batches

Twelve to twenty of a shape at a time. If a glaze misbehaves on a batch, we say so and it becomes a seconds batch.

Kiln to door in five days

Orders are packed the day a firing comes out and posted in shredded cardboard. No bubble wrap, no polystyrene.

Seconds, not landfill

A wonky rim or a run of glaze does not make a pot useless. Seconds are sold at the studio at 30% off, never binned.

Made to be used

Everything is dishwasher and microwave safe. Chips are rare and, when they happen, we will glaze and refire a piece for you.
