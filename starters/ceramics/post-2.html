Every product photo on this site is of the batch that is actually for sale. When that batch sells out, we photograph the next one and change the picture. It sounds like a lot of work. It is.

Because the kiln has opinions

We fire in reduction, which means starving the kiln of oxygen for the last few hours so the flame pulls oxygen out of the glazes instead. That is what turns iron into rust-red and copper into flashes of pink. It also means the same Ash glaze can come out silvery in one firing and moss-green in the next, depending on where the pot sat and how the weather was.

A catalogue photo from two years ago would be a lie, or at least a hope. We would rather show you the shelf.

How we do it

  1. The kiln opens on a Thursday morning and everything goes on the long table by the window.
  2. We sort into firsts, seconds and the bucket. The bucket is for the ones that cracked.
  3. Firsts are photographed in daylight, one shape at a time, on the same grey board.
  4. The photos go up here the same evening, along with the count of what is in stock.

What that means when you order

The piece you get is one of the pieces in the photo. Not a similar one, one of those. If we are down to the last three of a shape, the listing says so. If a batch came out with a glaze we are not happy with, it does not go on the site at all: it goes to Seconds Saturday.

"The photo is a promise about this batch, not a wish about the next one."

Something we say a lot in the studio
