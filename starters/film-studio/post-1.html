An advert asks for thirty seconds of your attention and gives you a price. A film asks for three minutes and gives you a reason. Most brands can afford the first and badly need the second. This is the case for the second.

What an advert is for

Adverts are built to interrupt. They live in the gaps between things people actually want to watch, so they have to shout, and they have to shout the offer. There is nothing wrong with that. It is a job, and someone has to do it.

What a film is for

A brand film is not an interruption. Nobody has it forced on them. They chose it, and that changes everything about how it can talk. It can be quiet. It can take its time. It can show one person doing one true thing and trust the audience to understand why that matters.

The feeling comes first

People remember how a film made them feel long after they have forgotten what it said. A good brand film aims for the feeling and lets the facts follow. If you are still explaining features in the first minute, you are making an advert with a longer runtime.

How to tell which one you need

  • If the goal is a sale this month, make an advert.
  • If the goal is that people know who you are next year, make a film.
  • If you cannot say what your brand believes in one sentence, make the film first. It will tell you.

Nobody shares an advert with a friend. People share films.

Overheard at a wrap party, and true

The honest caveat

A film costs more than an advert and it pays back more slowly. It is an investment in being known rather than in being clicked. Brands that can hold their nerve for a year tend to make one film and then never go back.
