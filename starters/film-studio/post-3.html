A hero film is the one people watch. A cutdown suite is the set of shorter versions that do the work everywhere else. Most brands pay for the first and forget to plan the second, and then wonder why the film disappeared after launch week. This is a guide to getting the suite right.

The lengths

We deliver every hero film with four shorter cuts, and we make them on the same shoot day from the same footage, so they share the same light and the same feeling.

Cut for the platform, not from the film

Sound off

Most social cuts play silent. If the 30-second version needs the voiceover to make sense, it does not work. We plan the shorter cuts around pictures that carry the meaning on their own, and we burn in captions properly, not as an afterthought.

The first second

A hero film can open on a quiet wide shot and earn its way in. A six-second sting cannot. Each cut has its own opening frame, chosen for the place it will be seen, and often it is not a frame from the start of the film at all.

What to ask your studio

  • Are the cutdowns planned before the shoot, or made from whatever is left?
  • Do the short cuts work with the sound off?
  • Is there a version framed for vertical, shot as vertical rather than cropped?
  • Who owns the project file, so cuts can be remade next year?

The hero film is the argument. The cutdown suite is showing up to make it, every day, everywhere.

The short version

Plan the suite in the brief. Shoot for it on the day. Deliver it with the hero, not after. Then the film keeps working long after the launch week, which is the whole point of having made one.
