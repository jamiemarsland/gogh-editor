Most brand films are shot in a single day. That is not a compromise; it is a discipline. A well-planned day gives you everything a well-funded week would, minus the drift. Here is how ours run, hour by hour, and why.

Before the day

The day is won in the fortnight before it. Locations are visited at the hour we will shoot them. Every scene has a shot list, every shot has a purpose, and every person on set knows which of the five cuts a shot is for. By the morning of the shoot, nobody is deciding anything for the first time.

The day itself

07:00 — the walk

Before any kit comes off the van, the director and the DoP walk the location with the light. Ten minutes. It changes the order of the day more often than not.

08:00 to 12:00 — the hard scenes

Interviews and anything with dialogue happen while people are fresh and the light is kind. The emotional heart of the film is in the can before lunch.

13:00 to 17:00 — the pictures

The afternoon is for the shots that carry the short cuts: hands, places, details, the six-second moments. This is also when the plan bends, because something better always turns up.

After the day

  • Footage is backed up twice before the van leaves.
  • The editor sees selects within 48 hours, while the day is still fresh.
  • A rough cut of the hero arrives in a week; the cutdowns a week after that.

One day, planned properly, beats three days, planned loosely. Every time.

What it costs you

One day of your team's time, one location, and a brief that was honest. In exchange you get a hero film and its suite, delivered in twenty-one days, made by people who were awake for all of it.
