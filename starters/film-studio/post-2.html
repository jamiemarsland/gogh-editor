Every production we have ever made started the same way: a conversation with no camera in the room. What gets said in that first hour decides more about the film than anything that happens on set. Here is what we ask, and why.

Who is this for?

Not the demographic. The person. We ask clients to describe one real human being who should see this film and feel something. A name helps. Once there is a person in the room, every later decision gets easier: the tone, the length, the music, the place.

What should they feel?

Not what should they know. We will get to that. The first question is the emotional target: relief, pride, curiosity, a little envy. A film with a clear feeling can carry a lot of information. A film without one cannot carry any.

What is true here?

Every brand has one thing about it that is simply, verifiably true, and it is rarely the thing on the homepage. It is the founder who still answers support emails. The factory that has never missed a Friday. We look for the true thing, because that is what people can feel through a screen.

What we do not ask

  • We do not ask what the competitors are doing. It leads to films that look like everyone else's.
  • We do not ask for a mood board yet. Pictures arrive too early and decide too much.
  • We do not ask about the budget until we know what the film is. Then we talk about it honestly.

A brief is not a list of things to include. It is one thing you refuse to leave out.

What happens next

We go away and write a page. One page, no pictures, describing the film as if it already existed: what you see, what you hear, how it ends. If that page makes you feel the thing we agreed on, we have a film. If it does not, we have saved you a shoot.
